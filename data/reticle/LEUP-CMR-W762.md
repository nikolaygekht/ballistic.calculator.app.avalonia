# LEUP-CMR-W762 — Leupold CMR-W 7.62 Illum. FFP

The 7.62 sibling of [LEUP-CMR-W556](LEUP-CMR-W556.md): the same red horseshoe, the same 1 mil hash
scale and mil ruler, but a **ten-rung ladder out to 1200 m** instead of seven to 900. **First focal
plane**, so the subtensions hold at every magnification.

Framed **to the 20 mil mark** (42 × 28 mrad). The etched arms carry on to about ±39.7 mil and are closed
by 3.00 mil heavy field-stop blocks; the spine likewise continues to roughly 24 mil below the axis. Both
run off-frame here so the ladder is not dwarfed. Leupold's sheet draws **two** field-of-view circles, 6×
and 8×, which is its own way of saying no single frame is the whole reticle.

## The pattern

- **Red horseshoe** — 2.18 mil OD, 1.46 mil ID, open at the bottom, with a **0.15 mil red dot**.
- **Post in the horseshoe gap** — its tip is the **300 m hold**.
- **Horizontal hash scale** — a hash every **1 mil** to ±10, numbered every 2; fine lines **0.20 mil**.
- **Medium arms** — **1.00 mil** thick from ±10.4 mil outward, with taller ticks at **15 and 20 mil**.
- **Mil ruler** — vertical, above the left arm at x −10.1 mil, ticks on whole milliradians 1…5.
- **Drop ladder** — a crossbar at each published range 400…1200 m, narrowing with distance, with
  unnumbered half-range ticks between them. Every hundred is numbered, unlike the 5.56.

### The zero: 50 m published, 100 m in practice

Leupold states a **50 m zero**. Tested in this application the ladder matches at a **100 m zero**, so
100 m is what to enter.

This is the **same discrepancy as [LEUP-CMR-W556](LEUP-CMR-W556.md)**, which also lines up at 100 m
rather than the 50 m Leupold publishes. Two different cartridges, two different ladders, the same
offset — so it is systematic rather than a coincidence of one load, and it suggests Leupold's "50 metre
zero" describes how the reticle is meant to be set up on the rifle rather than the line the drop table
was computed from. Leupold publishes neither sight height nor atmosphere, so it cannot be settled from
the sheet. What is settled is which zero makes the labels land on the etched ranges.

Half-range ticks sit at 1.88 / 3.05 / 4.38 / 5.89 / 7.60 / 9.56 / 11.81 / 14.39 / 17.36 mil — the 350
through 1150 m holds.

From 500 m out the crossbars work out at a near-constant **0.45 m at their own range** (0.92 × 500,
0.75 × 600, 0.46 × 900 … all land near 450 mm). The **400 m bar breaks that rule**, measuring 0.85 mil
where 1.1 would fit it — it is drawn narrower than its neighbours, and that is what the sheet shows
rather than an error here.

## What is not reproduced

The etched reticle carries a **christmas tree of wind holds** — nine rows (400…1200 m), with **10 and
20 mph** marks each side as 0.20 mil squares, and a bracket at each row's left end spanning **12 in at
that row's range**. Note this differs from the 5.56, which carries four wind columns (5/10/15/20 mph)
rather than two. Only the mark sizes are published, never their positions, so the tree is omitted. The
drop ladder is complete.

## Which numbers came from where

Published: the ten drop values, all three line weights, the horseshoe diameters, the dot, and the
1.00 mil ruler spacing. Measured off the sheet: crossbar widths, half-range tick positions, the 15/20 mil
outer ticks.

The sheet was proven to scale on its own numbers before anything was measured from it — its hash axis
gives 41.0 px/mil, its ladder 41.4 px/mil against the published 18.93 mil at 1200 m, and **all ten**
published drops land within 0.1 mil of their drawn rows. Each Leupold sheet is checked this way
separately; sharing a template with the 5.56 is not on its own evidence.

## Known label overlap

The **300 m** anchor sits close to the hash-numeral row, so its distance label clips one numeral. The
other nine are clear.
