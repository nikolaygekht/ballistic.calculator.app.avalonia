# SPECTER-7.62 — Elcan Specter DR 1-4×, 7.62

The 7.62 sibling of the 5.56 file, framed **at 4×** (140 × 165 MOA), with a stadiametric rangefinder
in the lower left.

## The pattern

Heavy duplex posts in from the field stop, a fine centre cross whose intersection is the 100 m zero,
then eight rungs for **300…1000 m** in two forms:

- **300–600 m** — crossbars on the fine spine, tapering inward.
- **700–1000 m** — aiming circles with flanking bars, each about **1.4 m across at its own range** —
  the same convention as the 5.56 file.

The spine is drawn **straight**, as the glass is etched. Spin drift is real but not compensated: hold
**0.6 MOA right at 700 m rising to 1.1 MOA at 1000 m** (1:12 RH).

The etched glass reads **100–1000 m with 7.62 M80** and **100–600 m with the 7.62 carbine**, which
shares the 300–600 m crossbars; the numerals past 6 are M80 only. Only the M80 ladder is reproduced
here — the carbine has no separate marks to draw.

The library entry's 810 m/s is a 21.6 in barrel. The ladder is cut for 830 m/s — nominal M80 from a
22 in barrel — so raise it and the labels land on the etched ranges. Left at 810 m/s the same marks
label 290 / 387 / 485 / 582 / 677 / 775 / 875 / 972 m, which is correct arithmetic for a slower load,
not an error.

## The rangefinder

Four bars at lower left, left-aligned on a common reference, each **76 cm (30 in)** — NATO torso
width — at 300 / 400 / 500 / 600 m, so **8.71 / 6.53 / 5.23 / 4.35 MOA**. Fit the target's width
between the reference line and a bar end to read its range. Per the legend on the glass it is valid at
**4×**, which is also the magnification this whole file is framed for.

## How the load was identified

830 m/s is what identifies it. Against a user's photograph of the glass measuring **4.6 / 8.1 / 12.3 /
17.3 / 24.3 / 32.6 / 40.5 / 51.0 MOA**, the computed ladder fits to **0.49 MOA RMS** with no trend. At
the library entry's own 810 m/s the residual is 1.73 MOA and grows with range — the signature of a
ladder cut for a flatter trajectory.
