# LEUP-CMR2 — Leupold Illum. CM-R²

A red horseshoe over a seven-bar drop ladder, with a short hashed horizontal arm each side. The
best-documented sheet in this folder: every dimension is printed, in mil *and* MOA, so nothing here is
measured or inferred except one bar width.

## Second focal plane — read this first

Leupold states the CM-R² "will measure (subtend) correctly when the riflescope is set to the **highest
available magnification**", and the sheet is drawn for the **Patrol 6HD**, so these subtensions are the
6× ones. At any other magnification the marks subtend something else and the sight picture here does not
apply. That is why `at 6x` is in the reticle's name.

## The pattern

- **Red horseshoe** — 1.75 mil OD, 1.50 mil ID (a 0.125 mil ring), open at the bottom, with a **0.15 mil
  red dot** at the aiming point.
- **Horizontal arms** — fine 0.15 mil lines out to **7.65 mil** each side, each closed by a heavier T
  post, with **vertical hashes at 2.65 and 5.00 mil** standing 0.28 mil tall.
- **Drop ladder** — seven bars below the centre, each **0.5 m (about 20 in) wide at its own range**, so
  the bar brackets a torso at the distance it stands for. Numerals 4, 6 and 8.

Leupold's sheet dimensions the geometry completely and says nothing whatever about the load behind it.
That is less of a problem than it sounds: the application labels every mark from **your** trajectory, so
the ladder is usable with any load — it simply cannot be checked against a manufacturer's intent.

## The marks

| Hold below centre | Bar width | Range it stands for |
|---|---|---|
| 0.87 mil | 1.67 mil | 300 |
| 1.89 mil | 1.25 mil | 400 |
| 3.20 mil | 1.00 mil | 500 |
| 4.95 mil | 0.83 mil | 600 |
| 6.98 mil | 0.71 mil | 700 |
| 9.60 mil | 0.63 mil | 800 |
| 12.51 mil | 0.56 mil | 900 |

**How the ranges were established.** The sheet prints the six widths 1.25 … 0.56 mil but never says which
bar is which range. Width × range is a constant 500 across all six — every bar subtends 0.5 m at its own
distance — which fixes 1.25 mil as 400, 1.00 as 500 and so on down to 0.56 as 900. The numerals 4, 6 and
8 then land on the 400, 600 and 800 bars, which is the check that the assignment is right.

Two honest gaps:

- The **300 m bar's width is not printed**. 1.67 mil is what the same 0.5 m rule gives, and it is the one
  dimension in this file that Leupold did not supply.
- The sheet does not say whether the numerals are hundreds of **metres or yards**. The bar-width rule
  works out at 0.5 m per range in metres and 18 in per range in yards — both are plausible torso widths,
  so the drawing cannot settle it. It does not affect use, since the application labels from your own
  trajectory.

## Wind holds

None. This reticle has no wind marks at all — the horizontal hashes at 2.65 and 5.00 mil are what you
hold with, and they are dimensioned.
