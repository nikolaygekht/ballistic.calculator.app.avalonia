# ACOG-TA44-556mm — Trijicon ACOG 1.5×16S, RTR™ .223

The .223 member of the RTR family on the compact 1.5×16S ACOG (**TA44-C-400390**): the same horseshoe
and dot as [the 9 mm version](ACOG-TA44-9mm.md), a flatter four-rung ladder, and a pair of dots on the
horizontal axis that the 9 mm reticle does not have. Fixed 1.5×.

## The pattern

- **Horseshoe** — **18.1 MOA outer diameter**, **1.2 MOA** thick (the 9 mm's is 1.0), open at the bottom.
- **Aiming dot** — **2.0 MOA**, red, at the centre. This is the zero.
- **Two dots on the horizontal axis** — 2.0 MOA, at **±16.1 MOA**. Present only on the .223 sheet.
- **Ladder** — four crossbars, **0.44 MOA** thick on a 0.44 MOA spine. Numerals **4** and **6**, 5.0 MOA
  tall, 10 MOA out from the spine.

## The marks

| Drop below the dot | Range | Bar width | Labelled at (XM855, 100 m zero) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6.5 MOA | 400 m | 4.15 MOA | 384 m |
| 11.5 MOA | 500 m | 3.32 MOA | 502 m |
| 17.9 MOA | 600 m | 2.77 MOA | 618 m |
| 26.1 MOA | 700 m | 2.37 MOA | 728 m |

The **drops are printed** on Trijicon's sheet. The **widths are not** — unlike the 9 mm sheet, which
dimensions every bar — so they are the family's own rule applied at these ranges: 19 in at the bar's own
distance, the rule the 9 mm sheet establishes to within 1% and the 18.1 MOA horseshoe (19 in at 100 yd)
confirms.

### Metres, unlike the 9 mm sibling

This reticle is graduated in **hundreds of metres**, established by running it in the application: with
`.223/XM855 Ammo` at a **100 m zero** the four marks label **384 / 502 / 618 / 728 m** against the etched
400 / 500 / 600 / 700 — within 4% at every rung.

That is worth spelling out because the [9 mm RTR](ACOG-TA44-9mm.md) is in **yards**, proven by its own
printed bar widths. The two sheets share a layout but not a unit, and the .223 sheet prints no widths, so
nothing on it fixes the unit — reading the family as uniformly yards was wrong. The deeper marks are what
give it away: at 700 **yd** M855 needs about 20 MOA, where the sheet etches 26.1, while 700 **m** wants
about 24 MOA. Only the metric reading tracks the ladder all the way down.

## The two axis dots

The sheet dimensions them — 2.0 MOA at ±16.1 MOA — and says nothing about their purpose. They are not
wind holds in any documented sense, they are not a ranging pair with a stated target size, and the 9 mm
RTR has no equivalent. They are reproduced here as drawn and labelled as unexplained; do not read a
meaning into them that Trijicon has not published.

## Nothing was measured

The sheet is stamped "**\*Reticles not drawn to scale**". Every position in this file is a printed
dimension; the four bar widths come from the family's printed 19-inch rule, not from the drawing.
