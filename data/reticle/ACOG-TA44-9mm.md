# ACOG-TA44-9mm — Trijicon ACOG 1.5×16S, RTR™ 9 mm PCC

Trijicon's Rapid Target Reticle for a 9 mm pistol-calibre carbine, on the compact 1.5×16S ACOG
(**TA44-C-400389**): a horseshoe bracketing a central dot, over a four-rung ladder. Fixed 1.5×, so there
is only one magnification and one set of subtensions.

## The pattern

- **Horseshoe** — **18.1 MOA outer diameter**, **1.0 MOA** thick, open at the bottom with a **9.1 MOA**
  gap. At 1.5× the horseshoe is the fast close-range aiming reference.
- **Aiming dot** — **2.0 MOA**, red, at the centre. This is the zero.
- **Ladder** — four crossbars, **0.44 MOA** thick, on a 0.44 MOA spine, each **19 in wide at its own
  range**. Numerals **2** and **3** are 5.0 MOA tall, 10 MOA out from the spine.

## The marks

| Drop below the dot | Range | Bar width |
|---|---|---|
| 10.9 MOA | 150 yd | 12.1 MOA |
| 19.0 MOA | 200 yd | 9.1 MOA |
| 28.3 MOA | 250 yd | 7.3 MOA |
| 38.6 MOA | 300 yd | 6.1 MOA |

Every one of those eight numbers is **printed on Trijicon's sheet** — this reticle is fully dimensioned,
positions and widths both.

## Yards, not metres — and how that is known

This sheet settles the unit **for this reticle** — and only this one. Each bar is a **19 in bracket at its
own range**, the same convention Trijicon uses on the VCOG, and the arithmetic only works in yards:

| Bar | Width × range (yd) | Implied target |
|---|---|---|
| 150 yd | 12.1 × 1.5 × 1.047 | 19.0 in |
| 200 yd | 9.1 × 2.0 × 1.047 | 19.1 in |
| 250 yd | 7.3 × 2.5 × 1.047 | 19.1 in |
| 300 yd | 6.1 × 3.0 × 1.047 | 19.2 in |

Read as metres the same widths imply a 20.8 in target — high by exactly the yard/metre ratio (1.0936),
which is the signature of the wrong unit rather than a different target. So the numerals are hundreds of
**yards**, and the unnumbered bars are 150 and 250 yd.

The horseshoe corroborates it: **18.1 MOA is 19 in at 100 yd**, so the horseshoe brackets the same
target at the zero distance, and its **9.1 MOA gap is 19 in at 200 yd** — the same width as the 200 yd
bar. The pattern is one 19-inch target measured at five distances.

**Do not carry this conclusion to the .223 sibling.** [ACOG-TA44-556mm](ACOG-TA44-556mm.md) is graduated
in **metres**, confirmed by running it against XM855. The two sheets share a layout but not a unit, and
the .223 sheet prints no bar widths, so it carries no evidence of its own — assuming the family was
uniformly yards was an error.

Trijicon publishes the geometry and nothing about the load. As always the application labels the marks
from your own trajectory, so a 115, 124 or 147 gr load will each relabel this ladder appropriately.

### What zero is this cut for? Undetermined

The sheet states no zero, and the ladder cannot settle it because the answer moves with the unstated
load. Working the first mark (**10.9 MOA at 150 yd**) backwards with a 2.6 in sight height:

| Load assumed | 100 yd zero | 50 m zero |
|---|---|---|
| 124 gr at ~1200 ft/s | ≈7.6 MOA | ≈12.0 MOA |
| 147 gr subsonic at ~1000 ft/s | ≈9.3 MOA | ≈16.1 MOA |

10.9 falls between the columns — a 50 m zero fits a 124 gr load, a 100 yd zero fits a heavy subsonic. Both
are plausible for a 9 mm PCC, and 50 m is the more common convention for the type. Those figures are
estimates, not engine runs, so treat the zero as **unknown**: pick the one that puts the labels on
150/200/250/300 yd for the load you actually shoot. The .223 sibling *is* pinned down — 100 m, verified
against XM855 — but it is a different cartridge on a different unit, so it says nothing about this one.

## Nothing was measured

The sheet carries Trijicon's standard footer, "**\*Reticles not drawn to scale**", so no dimension here
comes from measuring the drawing — every value is a printed number, and the bar widths that are *not*
printed do not exist in this file. Compare the Leupold sheets, which are drawn to scale and can be
measured once proven so.

## Wind holds

None — the RTR has no wind marks.
